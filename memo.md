# Memo: why CSAT slid, and who not to retrain
To: Priya Raman, Head of CX  |  From: Saarang P  |  Re: CSAT dashboard

**Short answer.** The slide is not an agent problem. It comes from one product: Pulse 2 earbuds made in twelve manufacturing lots (codes PL2-2510-1 to PL2-2512-4, ordered 23 Oct 2025 to 17 Apr 2026). Take those tickets out and CSAT is flat at about 3.4 to 3.5 in every month from January 2025 to June 2026, including the festive season. I would not spend the Rs 4 lakh training budget on the bottom ten.

**What the data shows**
- Those lots produced 2,563 tickets, 22% of everything in the 18 months. Customers on them scored 2.7; everyone else scored 3.5.
- 48% of orders from those lots led to a replacement, against about 8% for other Pulse 2 lots. Replacements rose 100% for this reason, not festive volume; volume rose too, but the lots explain the spike.
- Excess cost is about Rs 17.8 lakh: roughly 800 extra replacements at Rs 1,820 each (unit cost Rs 1,480 plus Rs 340 logistics, per policy, not Rs 2,500) and about 1,150 extra contacts at Rs 290. Q1 2026 alone was about Rs 12.7 lakh.
- On the Finance question: policy puts a replacement at Rs 1,820 for Pulse 2, not Rs 2,500. Volume was up, but it does not explain a doubling.

**The bottom ten, honestly**
- The raw bottom ten has six Escalations & Warranty agents. Policy says they are not compared with Tier 1, and they take the worst cases.
- The other four are the chat hardware rota (Kapoor, Pandey, Trivedi, Khanna), as Neha warned. On tickets not from the bad lots their CSAT matches their colleagues. On hardware-category chat tickets from the bad lots they handled 355 against 241 for other chat agents, and scored 2.1.
- With bad-lot tickets removed, the 38 Tier-1 agents sit between 3.3 and 3.65, with margins of about plus or minus 0.2. No one is a statistically clear retraining case.
- Top five for the Diwali bonus: do not use raw CSAT either, because queue mix decides it. The table in the dashboard shows adjusted scores.

**What I recommend**
1. Quarantine the remaining stock of those lots and ask the manufacturer for a root cause. 96% of the replacements came after 8 December 2025, when a weekly lot check would already have fired. That is about Rs 17 lakh that was avoidable.
2. Run the lot check weekly (the tool does it in seconds, no cost per ticket).
3. Hold the training budget. If you want to spend part of it, use it on the Pulse 2 triage rota (scripts for battery and charging faults), not on named people.

**Target.** Keep any lot's replacement rate under 12% (it reached 48%) by flagging a lot within three weeks of its first excess replacements. At the incident's size that protects about Rs 12 lakh a quarter.

**What I am less sure of.** The cause (hardware fault in the lots) is inferred from the replacement rate and agent notes, not confirmed. Roughly 1 in 20 tickets without an order number may be linked to the wrong lot. 13 junk IVR rows were left in.
